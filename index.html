How it works

  1. Spot check. Hold your phone where you'd record and stay quiet for 10 seconds. Repeat in 2–5 spots.
  2. Clap test. One loud clap shows how echoey the room is.
  3. Your result. The best spot, what's causing noise, and simple fixes. Saved as this room's baseline.

We don't promise silence. We promise better quality. Soundcheck fixes the problem at the source, so you don't need heavy cleanup that makes voices sound robotic.

Set up

Before you start

  • Leave the room the way it'll be when you record: fan, AC and windows as usual. We want to hear the real thing.
  • Hold the phone where your mouth would be, mic end pointing at you.
  • Stay silent during each 10-second check.
  • Take off headphones or external mics. Soundcheck tests the phone's own mic.

Audio is analysed on your phone and never leaves it.

Spot check

Record from at least 2 spots. Try a corner, the middle of the room, near the wardrobe and away from the window.

Length
10

Stay quiet…

Your mic isn't picking anything up. Check the mic isn't blocked or muted.

Clap test

Stand in your best spot so far. When you see CLAP!, clap once, sharp and loud, then stay still until the timer ends.

Echo is how long sound keeps bouncing around after you stop. Too much makes vocals sound distant and boxy.

My rooms

Each check is saved on this phone as that room's baseline. Check the same room at different times of day to find your quietest time.

    How Soundcheck measures

    Relative, not absolute. Phone mics aren't calibrated, so Soundcheck compares spots measured with the same phone, in the same session. "6 dB quieter" is reliable. A raw decibel reading would not be, so we don't show one.

    Background level is the typical (median) loudness while you stay silent. Sudden noises are moments more than 10 dB above that, like horns, doors or voices. Steady hum is a strong tone below 300 Hz, typical of fans, AC units and fridges.

    Echo is a rough estimate of reverberation time (RT60) from your clap's decay. As a rule of thumb, under about 0.4 s suits vocals. It's a guide, not a lab measurement.
